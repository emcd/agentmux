# Implementation Tasks

The spec deltas describe the whole contract. This file draws the phase line.

**Phase 1 is the 0.9.0 core.** **Phase 2 is 0.9.x follow-on.** The line is where
it is because the authorization guard cannot be deferred past the `Authorized`
state: quota releases only at the terminal transition, so an unowned `Authorized`
entry leaks quota permanently and blocks the target FIFO.

Validate default, `--features pty`, and the ACP paths independently at every
checkpoint — `src/pty/**` is behind a feature no default gate builds, and the
pre-commit `cargo-clippy-pty` hook is file-scoped.

- [x] Implement authorization as a relay-local transition that creates the batch's owner in the same atomic operation
- [x] Implement the relay-owned guard: keyed on `(batch, member, attempt)` at authorization, atomically bound to `PackingUnit ID` at partition. Creation at authorization is done, and the binding is a real `PackingUnit ID` recorded before the first target-side effect rather than a boolean standing in for one. All three coder transports declare their own through `PartitionSink` — Tmux at `transport.rs` group construction, ACP at its prompt write, Pty per envelope — so the partition is reported by the only layer that can see it, and a budget group being one unit makes a multi-member unit real rather than hypothetical. Pty stays one member per unit by commitment, not by omission. The trailing "what remains is the partition itself" was left behind by the sink landing and contradicted the sentence after it; nothing remained
- [x] Implement the single terminal CAS, releasing admission quota on that transition and nowhere else
- [x] Implement the guard resolution order once: unit record if present, else `not_submitted` for a member never bound to a unit, else `submission_unknown`
- [x] Route **every** post-authorization pre-partition refusal through the guard's evidence order instead of overriding it. Four sites, found by mapping every explicit-outcome site in the delivery path to a disposition rather than trusting an enumeration: the relay's `prepare_coder_write` error arm, Tmux's `mailw` refusal (one arm covering both `transport_not_started` and `tmux_delivery_thread_stopped`), Tmux's `paste_group` pane-resolution failure, and Tmux's `enqueue` channel-full arm for envelopes. The relay site gained `complete_task_refusal`, taking its **outcome** from the guard and its **reason** from the refusal, so a member that turned out to be bound reports `submission_unknown` rather than a non-delivery claim the call cannot support, while "no such member" still reaches the sender. The transport sites report `not_submitted` directly, which is the evidence they hold: they refuse before any declaration, so nothing was written and it is provable rather than inferred. **Deliberately excluded because binding is the discriminator** — Pty's `write_all` failure, Tmux's `raww` and both `deliver_raw` arms, Tmux's `enqueue` raw arm, and the post-inject fan-out are all bound before their refusal. Routing through the guard rather than widening `reconcile_with_evidence` was the right seam: reconciliation is one-directional, rewriting `Failed` to `submission_unknown` when the unit recorded it, and never derives `not_submitted` — the stronger claim these sites were throwing away. Each refusal's code and reason survive as the outcome's `reason_code`/`reason`. Compliance against `delivery-quiescence` as already written — a post-authorization refusal SHALL be a terminal evidence result, and a pre-partition refusal SHALL terminalize through the guard — so no spec delta; the spelling is sender-visible, and the two tests pinning `failed` on the `mailw` arm were updated
- [x] Implement the mandatory post-authorization execution watchdog bounded by `[delivery].submission-timeout-ms`, anchored at authorization. Arming REQUIRED the transport submission-evidence tasks below, and they hold: all three transports resolve their outcome future at the write boundary rather than at the end of the turn — ACP immediately after the framed `session/prompt` write, Tmux at the `inject_literal_text` invocation, Pty at the buffered `write_all`. ACP's respawn gap needs no exemption either, because `mailw` refuses synchronously with no live runtime rather than holding an authorized member across it. So the bound measures relay-side execution, as specified, and not the agent's inference
- [x] On elapse, initiate the generation fence and terminalize nothing yet; keep accepting unit evidence through the fence windows and terminalize every still-unresolved member through the evidence order at the fence verdict. The fence is advanced from the worker loop rather than awaited, so the collect arm keeps running through both observation windows and a member that resolves in time still reports its own evidence
- [x] Release quota and outcome barriers at terminalization, but release the target's FIFO, raw barrier, and replacement only on a positive fence verdict. Quota release was already bound to the terminal transition alone; what landed here is the other half — a positive verdict builds the replacement generation in place, and a negative one holds the registry entry so none can be elected
- [x] Make unwind, channel closure, task or thread exit, generation replacement, and graceful shutdown all route through that one order; no lifecycle path selects an outcome of its own. Audited against every outcome-selection site in the worker. Unwind resolves `CollectorPanic` and channel closure `ChannelClosed`, both from `collect_outcome`'s join arms; a transport task or thread exiting drops its outcome sender, which arrives as the same channel closure; generation replacement resolves `ExecutionBound` at the fence verdict; graceful shutdown resolves `GracefulShutdown` for the members it finds in flight. Every one of the five reaches `complete_task_outcome_from_trigger`, which takes no outcome and reads the evidence order. **The claim audited is narrower than "no explicit outcomes remain": it is that no *authorized* member's outcome is selected by the lifecycle path that reaches it.** The remaining explicit-outcome sites belong to members never authorized — the unreachable dwell, the `Pubsub` refusal, and both construction-failure drains sit before `authorize_member` or apply only to queued and held tasks; shutdown's three `dropped_on_shutdown` spellings are the task 41 policy path over provably `Pending` entries. Explicit outcomes for *authorized* members do exist, but they are synchronous refusals on the delivery path rather than lifecycle events, so they fall outside this audit; their inventory lives in task 26 and is not duplicated here
- [x] Make collectors carry guard keys rather than own resolution; remove the `JoinError` branch in `src/relay/delivery/dispatch/outcomes.rs` that returns without producing an outcome
- [x] Ensure outcome-notification failure is counted and recorded without blocking the terminal transition or the quota release. The ordering half was already structural — the terminal CAS and quota release run before any notification — so the work was the counting: both outcome-notification channels discarded their result with `let _ =`. A sender with no attached UI and a sender with no live worker are deliberately **not** counted, being ordinary states rather than failures; a disconnected UI, a draining worker, and an unreadable registry are
- [x] Scope authorization to admitted members, so relay-originated work is not refused for want of a reservation it never held. Found while building the positive control `agentmux:issues/relay/68` asked for: **every terminal-outcome receipt was being deleted before any write.** `authorize_batch` returns `None` unless every member holds a `Pending` ledger entry; a receipt bypasses admission by design and holds none; nothing on the authorization path consulted `AsyncDeliveryTask::admitted`. So the receipt resolved `not_submitted` with `delivery_batch_not_authorized` and the sender was never told its message had not arrived — in exactly the case the notice exists for. Reproduced end to end: a member resolving `delivery_target_unreachable` is followed immediately by its receipt resolving `delivery_batch_not_authorized`, with the sender's pane empty after ten seconds. Introduced by this arc's own gating and **never released** — `admission.rs` does not exist at `v0.8.0` and no tag contains `authorize_batch` — so no deployed relay is affected. The fix filters the authorization set by `admitted`, mirroring `declare_singleton_unit`, which already skips the partition step against the same flag and whose comment names this exact failure. The filter cannot live inside `authorize_batch`: the terminal transition removes the entry it resolves, so from inside the ledger "never admitted" and "already resolved" are the same observation, which is why the flag rides on the task. In production `admitted == false` means a receipt and nothing else — the three other sites setting it are inline test fixtures. Covered by `relay_send_async_delivers_a_receipt_for_a_non_delivered_outcome`, the positive control the suppression test had always lacked: asserting that a delivered outcome produces no receipt is satisfied by a relay that emits none at all, which is what was happening

### Scheduling

- [ ] Ensure no elapsed-time path can resolve a `Pending` entry whose target is reachable: it leaves that state only by authorization, positively observed transport teardown, sustained unreachability past `[delivery].unreachable-dwell-ms`, or graceful shutdown
- [x] Make the per-target FIFO guarantee explicit and tested: mail and raw as one order, defined as worker-enqueue linearization rather than request or admission order
- [x] Bound the handover against both components, stopping at whichever of envelope count or canonical payload bytes binds first. Landed as `HandoverWindow` in `dispatch/batch.rs`, gating the worker's receive arm and its authorization step. The finding that shaped it: the relay already submitted consecutively without waiting — the receive arm is gated on `held.is_none()` and outcomes collect in a `JoinSet` — so work was being handed over in groups under burst and what was missing was the *bound*. `HandoverDimensions` had one consumer, at admission, checking a single envelope, and `maximum_handover_dimensions` had no callers at all — nothing counted cumulative in-flight envelopes or bytes, so the relay could overrun a ceiling its transport had declared. A member that does not fit is **held**, not skipped, because the window is a prefix of the target's FIFO and letting a smaller member behind it pass would reorder the queue. **Coverage boundary:** the window's arithmetic is unit-tested inline (both components, inclusive ceiling, at-least-one floor, saturating addition, reopening on close), and the gate is proven live by inverting `has_room` to a constant false and watching five delivery tests fail. **Resolved 2026-08-12:** enforcing declared dimensions means a `Ui` target holds one member at a time; operator confirmed `envelopes_max: 1` is correct and needs no widening, since a human-facing surface is not plausibly throughput-bound on envelope count. Not an open question
- [x] Fix the batch's membership **before** authorizing any member of it, and authorize the set whole under one `BatchId`. Split from the bound above, since only the bound is contract-legal on its own. Landed as `authorize_batch` — all-or-none under one ledger lock, one `BatchId` for the set, an `AttemptId` per member — replacing the per-member `authorize` whose `Option` the worker discarded, so a refused authorization now stops the submission it used to be ignored ahead of. The worker's two submission sites collapsed into `submit_batch`, which runs the target's gate once per batch, fixes membership against a *scratch copy* of the handover window, and advances the real window only once authorization has accepted the set — a refused batch must leave no reservation behind, because that room returns only when the window closes, which is gated on flight the refused members never entered. A handover window is **not** a batch: it accumulates as members arrive, so its extent cannot stand in for a set authorized together, and sharing its `BatchId` would be absorption into an already-authorized batch, which `transport-abstraction` forbids by name. `relay.delivery.batch.authorized` records the set — the partition inscription says which members shared a *submission*, and only this says which the relay committed to at one instant. **This does not make multi-member packing units possible**, and every batch has exactly one member today, which is a property of the seam rather than of this mechanism: `mailw` takes one envelope, so a set authorized together becomes N invocations, and the coder transports publish `Busy` on accepting the first
- [ ] Keep an activity-advanced target unauthorizable, even when the later observation matches the prompt-readiness template
- [ ] Reschedule `Pending` entries to a new generation on respawn; never re-invoke `Authorized` entries
- [x] Resolve still-`Pending` members `dropped_on_shutdown` on graceful shutdown, and `Authorized` members from evidence. Both halves hold, and the split is structural rather than a rule the shutdown path remembers: a member becomes `Authorized` only after the readiness and health gates, so every task the shutdown path can still reach by another route — the held member, the queue drain, and a task received mid-shutdown — is provably `Pending` and takes the `dropped_on_shutdown` spelling. `Authorized` members are exactly those in flight, and `shutdown_drain` terminalizes them through the guard's evidence order at the fence verdict. A `Pending` entry taking a policy outcome is what the spec permits rather than a violation of the one order, which governs members whose delivery responsibility has already transferred

### Submission evidence

- [x] Add the typed evidence enum — `Submitted`, `NotSubmitted`, `SubmissionUnknown` — and map undifferentiated errors to `SubmissionUnknown`
- [x] Make partition deterministic and recorded to the guard before any target-side effect. Deterministic here means *settled and never revised* — at the moment of the write there is a definite answer to which members share it, and no later path re-derives one. It does not mean the partition is a function of the message set alone: a coalescing transport's group still depends on what had arrived when it drained its channel, which is inherent to coalescing and is exactly why the partition has to be reported rather than inferred. The mechanism is in: `PartitionSink` is the relay-injected seam a transport reports its partition through, and `declare` binds every proposed member or none, under the ledger lock, before the write. All three coder transports report through it — Tmux's budget group is declared between the token-budget split and the paste, ACP's between `batch_envelope_groups` and the framed `session/prompt`, Pty's per member immediately before its own `write_all`. `TransportImpl::reports_own_partition` now separates them from UI, which the relay still declares for. A member no unit covers is a real category rather than an edge case: a terminal-outcome receipt bypasses admission, so nothing declares one anywhere
- [x] Record one immutable per-unit evidence record before member fan-out, and resume fan-out from it after a panic. The record is unit-owned rather than copied per member — `LedgerState.units` holds one `UnitRecord` per live unit, written once before fan-out and read by every member that terminalizes, then dropped when the last of them does. Disagreement between siblings is no longer representable rather than merely avoided: there is one record and they all read it
- [x] Resolve an unbound member `not_submitted`, keyed on unit binding rather than on the manner of failure
- [x] Resolve every member from its own unit's record; remove group-wide outcome application. For a **bound** member the unit record is authoritative at terminalization, and its producer's independently computed outcome is discarded even where the two agree — agreement was never the property worth having, one record read by every member of the unit is, and that makes sibling disagreement unrepresentable rather than merely absent. **Authority is gated on binding, not on the evidence value**, and that is load-bearing: `resolve_from_evidence` returns `NotSubmitted` both for an unbound member (the guard's inference from absence) and for a bound member whose unit recorded it (a transport's report of what its write proved). Treating the first as authoritative would let a transport that wrote before declaring have its `delivered` replaced by a provable-non-delivery claim, inventing the one direction this contract exists to prevent — so `TerminalTransition::Won` carries `bound` alongside the evidence, free because `terminalize` already holds `entry.unit` under the same lock. Causal metadata deliberately does **not** come from the record, which holds evidence rather than causes: a producer's `reason` and a causal `reason_code` like `pty_write_failed` survive an overridden outcome, while a code that merely *labels* an outcome does not, since `delivered` sitting on a `submission_unknown` tells the sender two different things. A bound `dropped_on_shutdown` is overwritten like any other producer spelling; the unbound `Pending` case keeps its policy spelling *because* it is unbound, not by a special case. Untracked receipts are unaffected — no ledger entry, so no evidence to be authoritative with

### Fencing

- [x] Supervise the initial ACP bootstrap as its own task rather than awaiting it ahead of the delivery loop, so a worker whose agent never finishes its handshake still reaches its shutdown gate and emits a verdict; give every bootstrap ownership of the runtime it produces from creation through install-or-teardown and readiness publication, since aborting the awaiting task cancels none of that; drop the second, unsupervised establish route on `AcpTransport::startup`
- [x] Break the ACP startup readiness poll on a shutdown request, so a signal mid-startup does not cost the relay its full prime timeout for every member still to come
- [x] Retain every generation-owned submission and permission executor handle, including the agent child of every in-flight ACP bootstrap. Aborting the async wrapper does not cancel the `spawn_blocking` closure, so the closure publishes its child into a per-transport registry the moment it spawns — before the handshake that can hold it there — and clears it as it disposes of the runtime
- [x] Make forced termination a latched state rather than a traversal of what is published at that instant, on both ACP and Tmux. A child spawned or published after the traversal was never signalled at all, and an executor between invocations presented an empty slot; publication now inherits the latch under the same lock the traversal takes, and a request that loses a non-blocking lock attempt is served by whoever holds it
- [x] Implement the five-step fence state machine as the only fence protocol: cooperative stop request, bounded cessation observation, non-blocking forced termination, second bounded observation, verdict
- [x] Keep steps 1 and 3 distinct, so a cooperatively stoppable executor is never force-terminated
- [x] Make both observations non-blocking with their own clock, never a blocking join, each bounded by `[delivery].fence-observation-timeout-ms`
- [x] Add a generation termination primitive to the transport contract, contracted to *initiate* cessation of every generation-owned effect path and return without blocking
- [x] Implement step 3 per transport as initiation only: ACP and Pty signal the child to terminate; Tmux signals its owned client invocations, never the server; UI drops the generation's broadcaster handle and subscriber senders. ACP signals the child of every in-flight bootstrap as well as the steady-state one, which is what ends a handshake the relay has no operation timeout over
- [x] Observe the results in step 4 — child reaped, invocations exited, executor returned — never inside the step 3 call. A bootstrap's record outlives its client, and dropping that client kills and waits the child, so an empty bootstrap registry is a reaping observation rather than a signalling one. Step 3 itself takes no blocking lock and performs no wait on either transport: a Tmux invocation is reaped by polling `try_wait` with the child still reachable, and both the ACP child lock and its bootstrap registry are only ever attempted. What a failed attempt would have reached is served instead by the holder, which re-reads the latch *after* releasing — a check taken while still holding the lock leaves the window where the requester's attempt fails against a holder that has already looked. The ACP registry's handoff re-runs the whole traversal rather than serving the holder's own record, because overlapping bootstraps mean the holder that defeats an attempt is generally not the owner of what that attempt would have reached
- [x] Make a successful primitive invocation not itself acknowledge the fence; only observed cessation does
- [x] Make the fence positive only on observed cessation, and route both timeout and failure to the negative branch
- [x] Keep the fence negative when cessation is not observed: admit no replacement for that target, hold its raw barrier, record the condition, and still resolve every member through the guard. The registry entry *is* the no-replacement guarantee — registration is the election a spawner must win, so a fail-stopped entry that outlives its worker is what no second generation can start beside. `raww` needs no barrier of its own for the same reason: it reaches the target through that same lookup
- [x] Block replacement and raw ordering barriers until the fence is positive, while allowing `submission_unknown` to terminalize before it. Terminalization runs at the verdict whatever its sign, so an unknown is reported without waiting on a positive one; only replacement and further writes are held
- [ ] Make raw wait for target-side ordering safety rather than for outcome terminality, which is the consumer side of the barrier above
- [ ] Resolve a submission stopped by the fence before any effect as `not_submitted`
